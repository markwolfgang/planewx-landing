Why I Built a
Decision Support System
A pilot story about timing, pressure, and the tools I needed before the go/no-go was already decided.
Mark Wolfgang · Founder · TBM 900
planewx.ai
Code TBMOPA
I built PlaneWX so I would not kill myself and my wife.
I am still building it for that reason.
Where this starts
Learned to fly at 49.
Risk averse by nature.
  • I aspire to fly as professionally as possible. The margins are thin.
  • The impact of risks can be catastrophic.
  • I am a founder who started as a pilot with a planning problem.
The core problem
The timing is wrong.
  • The weather products we trust most for go/no-go get sharp about a day before departure.
  • Often inside the last 16 hours.
  • By then the hotel is paid, the rental is booked, and family is waiting.
The accurate forecast arrives after the decision that mattered.
What that creates
Pressure to make the flight happen.
  • Friends or family waiting on the other end.
  • Passengers traveling with you.
  • Canceling or moving the trip has already gotten expensive.
FAA
External pressures are a major causal factor in a majority of general aviation accidents.
Personal minimums
Standards we write before pressure shows up
  • No flight within 20 NM of a thunderstorm
  • No landing if clouds are lower than 1,000' AGL
  • No takeoff or landing above 15 kt crosswind
  • No known icing unless the airplane is certified for it
Under pressure
Minimums get rationalized
What we tell ourselves
  • Crosswind is 20 kts
  • Clouds are at 900'
  • Some icing, but I can steer clear
Ego takes over
  • I can handle it
  • It will not happen to me
  • I have gotten away with it before
Soon after my certificate
A beach week with no room to move
  • Strict Airbnb cancellation. Friends already there.
  • Night-before forecast did not give the clarity I wanted.
  • Too late to cancel or rearrange. Wait for the morning products.
Morning of
Storm on the path. Blue to the right.
  • Departing was fine. The threat was ahead.
  • I turned right toward blue sky and stayed there.
  • I would not turn left until it was safe. Fuel and airports as outs.
Everyone we held dear was relying on me to put pressure and ego aside, and be smart, in order to live.
The realization
If I knew 2 or 3 days out that Friday looked bad and Thursday looked clear…
leave Thursday.
What I found
Plenty of weather. Not enough synthesis.
  • Localized and big-picture products are abundant. Almost overwhelming.
  • Raw products. Synthesis and analysis left solely to the pilot.
  • I am not a meteorologist. Some of the most useful products are written for other mets.
So I built a loop
Retrieve. Synthesize. Score. Refresh.
  • Pull the latest products as they publish
  • LLM synthesizes the big picture along my route, at my altitude
  • WX Score from my personal mins and aircraft capabilities
  • Refresh when new products land. Trend early enough to move the trip
Three days out
“Honey, Friday morning is looking bad. We may need to leave Thursday afternoon.”
Objective 1
A high-quality briefing and a WX Score
What it does
  • Quantified weather risk vs your mins and airplane
  • Updates as forecasts and models publish
  • Insight up to 14 days out
8.7
out of 10
1,724 briefing feedbacks
Not perfect. Hard to get right. Continuously improving.
The briefing is one pillar. Airlines and charter are required to use a Safety Management System. Most of GA still improvises.
Objective 2
A living FRAT, not a paper exercise
  • Aware of your flight history and known aircraft issues
  • Points at environmental hazards and complex airspace
  • Identifies risks that stack, not just single factors
  • Goal: normalize a repeatable self-assessment before you fly
Stacked risk
Night arrival. Ceiling at your 1,000' mins. Mountains. You live in Kansas.
  • No night landing in 45 days
  • No high-elevation landing for a while
  • 1,500+ hours total time does not erase those gaps
Living FRAT Document the call. PlaneWX does not decide for you.

Objective 3
I only had 2 or 3 pilots on speed dial
  • I needed a second set of eyes before go/no-go.
  • They had to be free at 6 AM on a Saturday.
  • One knew my airplane. Another had more experience. Rarely both.
Need Help Now
SMS mentors who are available right now
  • Mentor sees the same briefing and the FRAT
  • Bill: KSQL to KSEZ, WX Score at 0%, vacation home waiting
  • A match was made. They talked. The flight was completed safely
  • You remain PIC. You still make the final call
Mentor network Same briefing. Same FRAT. A second set of eyes.
Still too rare
Mentor utilization is under 1% of confirmed flights.
I want to normalize mentors in general aviation. Almost everyone can use one on a low-scoring flight.
The three pillars
A Decision Support System
Pillar 1
Briefing + WX Score
Quantified risk early enough to move the trip
Pillar 2
Living FRAT
Stacked risk, tied to the flight you are about to fly
Pillar 3
Mentors
A second opinion when the picture looks wrong
The first pilot Decision Support System in general aviation
Aligned incentives
5X5 rewards preparation
  • Up to 10% on annual aircraft insurance for PlaneWX users
  • Privacy stays intact: no briefings, scores, or decisions shared
  • If you are already 5X5 insured, they only need a yes/no on PlaneWX use
Access
Free gets the same DSS pillars
  • Briefing, FRAT, and mentors are available free of charge
  • Paid is automation and advanced features, not the safety core
  • ~2,200 users in 35 countries. It works globally.
